Your pictures are everywhere.

Your holiday photos in Apple Photos. The RAWs on your camera card. Drone videos on a tiny microSD. And somewhere, there’s that USB stick from Mum.

Your old drive has folders called “Holiday”, “Holiday new” and “Pictures to sort”. The next drive holds a different selection. When you need that one photo, you no longer know where to start.

Give your pictures a place together ↓
TrueArchive for Mac

One place for all your pictures.

A drive with enough space. One folder for your archive. TrueArchive brings your pictures and videos there from Apple Photos, SD cards and selected folders – as a one-off import or on a schedule.

Your sources stay untouched. The archive belongs to you: ordinary files you can open even without TrueArchive.

Sorted. Described.
Found again.

New archives are organised by year, month and day, for example 2026/2026-09/2026-09-10. Only months and days containing files are created. Without a known capture date, Unbekannt (unknown) stays directly in the archive. How files are organized.

Originals, finished edits and available metadata stay together: capture dates, GPS coordinates, keywords and album memberships, depending on the source and format.

XMP files store this metadata using an open standard and sit alongside your pictures as companion files. TrueArchive imports and adds to existing XMP files, or creates them in the archive when none exist.

digiKam is a free, open-source photo management application for organising and searching your archived picture collection. Add your archive folder to it and search by date, keywords or location.

Once digiKam has indexed faces and you have assigned names, you can also find people. Like Mum, across pictures from all your drives.

Bring your pictures together

Many sources, one collection. You choose what goes into your archive.

Apple Photos, cards and drives
Import your library and selected local folders. In Apple Photos, you can narrow the capture date range.
Google Photos via Takeout
Manually import an extracted export with its supported metadata. How to prepare your export.
Once or on a schedule
Archive directly or check a preview first for local sources. Enable a schedule for Apple Photos and local sources; your Mac needs to be awake and TrueArchive running.

Keep originals and metadata

Your sources stay unchanged. Pictures and their stories stay together in the archive.

Originals, edits and picture information
Available details such as dates, GPS and albums come along. XMP companion files store metadata using an open standard; missing XMP files are created in the archive.
Recognize duplicates, keep different edits
Identify identical media files and record where they came from. Different Nitro edits are kept as variants when their metadata is compatible. Other conflicts remain open for review. More about variants and conflicts.

Find your pictures again

An archive you can open without TrueArchive and use with other applications.

Order on your drive
Ordinary files in date folders; new archives use a fixed year/month/day order. The free, open-source photo manager digiKam lets you search by date, places and keywords – and by people once faces have been indexed and named.
Add keywords automatically
If you enable it, Apple Vision adds keywords in German, English or both to photos and, separately, videos. For videos, one frame every ten seconds is analysed and sample timestamps are stored in XMP. Recognition runs on your Mac; existing keywords stay intact.
